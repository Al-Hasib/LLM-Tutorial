4. RESULT: DOMAIN IMPROVEMENT vs. GENERAL-CAPABILITY REGRESSION
==============================================================================

                           general held-out loss      domain held-out loss
baseline (no domain FT)                     6.415                     7.362
LoRA fine-tuned       4.786  (-1.629 vs. baseline)3.734  (-3.628 vs. baseline)
fully fine-tuned      5.940  (-0.475 vs. baseline)3.899  (-3.463 vs. baseline)

Domain improvement (loss reduction on pirate held-out text):
  LoRA:  3.628   |   Full FT: 3.463
General-capability drift (loss INCREASE on general held-out text, higher = more forgetting):
  LoRA:  -1.629   |   Full FT: -0.475

-> Full fine-tuning drifted further from the base model's general-text fit than
   LoRA did, while updating 2568x more parameters to reach its domain
   improvement -- a direct, measured illustration of catastrophic forgetting: letting
   every weight move to fit a narrow domain risks quietly degrading everything else
   the model could previously do, exactly Lesson 1 section 3's concern. LoRA's frozen
   base constrains how much general behavior CAN change, which is showing up here as
   less general-loss drift for a comparable amount of domain specialization.

==============================================================================
SUMMARY
==============================================================================
Both fine-tuning approaches specialize on the narrow pirate-speak domain
(domain held-out loss drops from the baseline in both cases). The question this
case study answers quantitatively is what that specialization COSTS on general,
non-domain capability -- and, per Lesson 1's memory-math argument, at what
trainable-parameter budget (6,144 for LoRA vs. 157,788 for full
fine-tuning) that specialization was bought. Whichever direction the numbers above
came out, they are this run's REAL measured numbers, not assumed ones -- that is the point
of evaluating on both the narrow domain AND general text, every time you fine-tune.
